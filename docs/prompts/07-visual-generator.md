## Design approach

Modern editorial advertising. Minimal, sophisticated, intentional and commercially usable. The composition should feel designed, not decorated.

Visual density must be LOW. Target approximately 35-50% intentional negative space. Use ONE dominant visual idea and no more than three major visual elements. Empty space is a design element: never fill it merely because it exists.

## Strict composition rules

Prefer:
- one main focal point
- generous negative space
- restrained brand accents
- clean hierarchy
- simple geometry only when it serves the concept

Avoid:
- collage layouts
- multiple panels
- floating cards
- excessive badges or stickers
- unnecessary icons
- decorative arrows
- random geometric ornaments
- fake interfaces
- excessive gradients
- multiple competing focal points
- excessive shadows or glow
- busy backgrounds
- tiny decorative typography
- visual clutter
- generic stock-photo clichés
- watermarks

Do not invent statistics, claims, labels, slogans, calls to action or decorative copy.

## Authenticity

Never invent a physical establishment, interior, employee, customer, facility, equipment or branded location and portray it as belonging to the client.

If the client is a physical establishment and no authentic location asset was supplied, do not create an imaginary version of that location. Use an abstract, typographic or neutral conceptual direction instead.

Generic lifestyle imagery may be used only when it is clearly conceptual and cannot reasonably be interpreted as the client's actual premises, staff or customers. Use neutral, non-identifiable environments and no third-party branding or signage.

## Visual-mode behavior

When the creative direction is TYPOGRAPHIC:
- photography is prohibited
- rely on typography, color, whitespace and subtle graphic structure
- use no people or locations
- treat the result like a sophisticated editorial poster, not an infographic

When the creative direction is ABSTRACT:
- do not depict a literal client location
- use restrained texture, light, shape or conceptual imagery derived from the brand
- keep abstraction secondary to the communication hierarchy
- do not make it futuristic unless Brand DNA explicitly supports that style

When the creative direction is GENERIC_LIFESTYLE:
- imagery represents a general concept, never the client's actual location, employees or customers
- use a neutral, non-identifiable environment
- avoid visible third-party brands and signage

## Quality bar

Follow the approved brand visual language and palette mood, but do not use every available brand color or motif at once.

The result should feel like work produced by a senior designer at a premium branding agency: elegant, confident, clean, editorial and easy to understand in less than two seconds on a phone.

Avoid the stereotypical AI-generated social-media-post look.
